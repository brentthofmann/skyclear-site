# SKYCLEAR — Give It a Texas Soul

Paste into Claude Code. The build looks premium now. This pass is about FEELING, not new
components. Right now the site sells services (lights/glass/solar). It should sell the Texas
LIFE those services belong to — pride, history, sport, family, the good evening at home.
Services become how you get there, not the pitch. Calm, confident, warm. The kind of site a
proud Texan homeowner sees and thinks "yeah, these are my people." Thread this through what
already exists. Report, don't chain.

## THE BALANCE (read first — this is the whole point)
Texan SOUL, modern CLEAN execution. The feeling is warm/proud/rooted; the container is
contemporary, minimal, professional. Apple-product-page restraint with a porch-light heart.
This site exists to DRIVE SALES and win clients — warmth builds trust, clean builds
credibility, and a proud homeowner needs both to call.
- YES: generous whitespace, crisp modern sans type, restrained palette, subtle motion,
  high-end photography, lots of breathing room. The Texas pride lives in the COPY, the
  IMAGERY (real golden-hour homes), and the team-color moment — not in the chrome.
- NO / avoid drifting old-school: no woodgrain/leather/rope textures, no rustic serif
  "saloon/ranch" fonts, no distressed/vintage badges, no flag overlays, no star-spangled
  borders, no "howdy/y'all" gimmicks, no kitsch. Pride is felt, never costume.
- Litmus test: it should look like a premium tech-forward home brand that happens to be
  proudly Texan — NOT a BBQ-joint or western-themed site. If a choice reads "theme park
  Texas," cut it.

## THE SHIFT
- Lead with the SCENARIO, support with the service. Imagery and copy should evoke: the porch
  glowing at dusk, the home looking its best for the neighborhood, Friday-night-lights team
  pride, family on the patio, a clean clear view out the window in the morning. Aspirational
  but grounded — not luxury-flashy, not loud. Texas calm and confident.
- Pace it slower and more cinematic. Generous spacing, unhurried scroll reveals, let images
  breathe. This is "settle in," not "buy now."

## COPY PASS (rewrite in place, keep CTAs/phone/areas)
Tone: warm, plainspoken, proud, a little poetic — never salesy or hype. Think how a
well-respected local company talks. Examples of the register (adapt, don't copy verbatim):
- A scenario-led line near the top of the lighting section, e.g. "When the sun drops over
  the Hill Country, your home is just getting started." Then the service explanation.
- Frame game-day as belonging/ritual, not a feature: lights in your team's colors when it
  matters, the whole street knowing whose house this is.
- Window/solar framed around pride of ownership and the clear Texas morning, not just "clean."
- Service area copy should sound rooted: "From Cypress to the Hill Country" / naming Austin,
  The Woodlands, San Antonio with a sense of place, not a dry list.
Keep it tight — a few strong lines beat paragraphs. No clichés like "lone star" overuse;
earn the pride, don't shout it.

## VISUAL/MOTION PASS (within current design)
- Imagery should favor warm golden-hour and blue-hour Texas exteriors, porches, homes
  looking lived-in and loved — real and specific, not glossy stock. Use the Pexels API
  (site/scripts/fetch-media.py) only where a real client asset isn't available; prefer
  Brent's real job photos.
- Soften motion: a touch slower and gentler on reveals (~0.7s ease-out), subtle parallax,
  nothing snappy or flashy. Calm = confidence.
- Keep the warm gold (`#E8B04B`) as the "evening light" through-line and clean blue
  (`#5FB4E8`) for glass/solar. Stay on the dark `#060A15` base.
- The Game Day scene = real Texas team colors (burnt orange + white default for Austin,
  optional Houston navy + red), framed as pride/ritual.

## DON'T
- Don't add gimmicks, don't get louder, don't restructure sections. This is mood + copy +
  image curation within the existing layout. Restraint is the point.

  pride, family) and the services land as the means to it.
- Looks MODERN, clean, premium — like a tech-forward home brand that's proudly Texan. Zero
  rustic/western/kitsch elements. Passes the "not a theme-park-Texas site" litmus test.
- Copy is warm, calm, plainspoken-proud — zero hype/salesy lines.
- Imagery is warm, real, golden/blue-hour, rooted in place.
- Motion feels unhurried and confident. Phone, areas, CTAs unchanged.
- The whole thing reads as built to win a serious buyer's trust and get the call.
